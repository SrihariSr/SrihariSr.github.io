Depth-Blind by Design

An Interpretability Autopsy of a Frozen Kepler Transit Classifier on TESS

NeurIPS 2026, Interp4Discovery workshop

Sole author

Accepted

[code]

Srihari Srinivasan. Depth-Blind by Design: An Interpretability Autopsy of a Frozen Kepler Transit Classifier on TESS. Interp4Discovery workshop, NeurIPS 2026.

The question. This paper takes a convolutional network trained on data from NASA's Kepler mission, freezes it, deploys it unchanged on data from its successor, TESS, and asks which stage of the pipeline fails when it falls short, and why.

The pipeline. Light curves are detrended and searched for periodic dips with Box Least Squares, then folded into a global and a local view and scored by a two-branch convolutional network following Shallue and Vanderburg (2018), trained on 6,726 Kepler Objects of Interest. On held-out Kepler data it reaches 0.945 PR-AUC, against 0.918 for an XGBoost baseline built on 19 hand-engineered statistics of the same views. Rule-based vetting and a four-check screen (secondary eclipses, known TESS systematic periods, period aliases and consistency across sectors) follow the network.

Three TESS populations. On 1,102 confirmed planets the frozen network recovers 62.2% at a score of 0.5 or more with both vetting rules passed. Across 4,002 unresolved TESS Objects of Interest it endorses 1,564 at a score of 0.9 or more; of the 30 highest ranked, 10 survive all four checks, and all 10 are already known candidates, so this is a positive control on the ranking rather than a discovery. Among 474 faint stars absent from the TESS Objects of Interest catalogue, no candidate survives screening.

What the analysis shows. A ten-seed ablation finds that the local view alone is statistically indistinguishable from the full network (Welch p = 0.83), so the global branch, 86% of the parameters, adds at most 0.005 PR-AUC at 95% confidence. On faint stars, 41.6% of detected periods fall within 10% of TESS's 13.7-day orbital period. Because each input view is normalised by its own minimum, the network sees the shape of a transit but not its depth, and its scores are indifferent to these systematics: a median of 0.030 near 13.7 days against 0.032 elsewhere.
