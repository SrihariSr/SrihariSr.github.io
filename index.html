Overview

I am a second-year undergraduate reading Computer Systems Engineering at the University of Warwick. I work on the systems side of machine learning: compilers, GPU and CPU kernels, and empirical studies of where trained models fail.

My sole-author paper on where a transit classifier trained on Kepler data fails when deployed unchanged on TESS was accepted to the Interp4Discovery workshop at NeurIPS 2026. I published my first paper, a sole-author IEEE conference paper on public perceptions of biometric privacy in the era of generative AI, while still at school in Dubai.

Alongside research I build tools from first principles, most of all Forge, a machine learning library and deep learning compiler with hand-written GPU kernels, released on PyPI.

Currently

  • Writing a matrix multiply kernel for Arm's Scalable Matrix Extension in Forge, aiming to outperform Apple Accelerate on the CPU.
  • Proposing a faster native Metal matrix multiply kernel to PyTorch; a prototype is with the maintainers for review.
  • Researching how to detect poisoned documents while a language model is being pretrained.
